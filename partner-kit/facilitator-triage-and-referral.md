# When a man is in distress: triage and referral

You lead education. You do not diagnose, counsel, or treat. This page is how
you hold that line and still take care of the man in front of you. It applies
in every setting and matters most where the load is heaviest: residential
programs, reentry, and cohorts of men back from deployment.

## What you watch for
Watch behavior, not labels. A man who goes silent and stays silent across
sessions. A man whose anger keeps rising past the practice. A man who stops
sleeping, stops eating, or says so. A man who talks about not being here.
You are not deciding what any of it means. You are deciding one thing: does
this man need more than a fathering course tonight.

## What you do, same day
1. Stay with him after the session. Two minutes, direct and calm: "I am glad
   you are here. Tonight I want to connect you with someone whose whole job
   is this part." No shame, no drama, no diagnosis.
2. Hand him to the organization's referral contact the same day. Every
   Certified Organization names that contact before its first cohort; it is
   on your cohort sheet. Walk him there or make the call together. A warm
   handoff, not a phone number on a card.
3. Tell your organization lead the same day. You never carry this alone.
4. If he speaks of harming himself or someone else, the organization's
   emergency protocol comes first and immediately. The course can wait.
5. He stays welcome. Getting help and finishing the course are not
   competitors. Most men do both.

## Courts, orders, and rebuilding trust
Rebuilding trust never overrides a court order. Where a no-contact or
protective order stands, the plan works within it: the man trains, the
record builds, and contact follows the court's terms and nothing else.
Never coach contact that an order forbids. If the arrangement is unclear,
the organization confirms it before the plan touches it.

## The line, stated once more
You will sit with men carrying real weight. Your job is a steady room, a
clear rhythm, and a fast, warm path to the people trained for the rest.
That is not a smaller job. It is the one that makes the others possible.
